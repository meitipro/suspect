---
title: "The three labels"
description: "EXPLAINS, CONTRADICTS and SILENT, and what each means."
---

The judge sees one hypothesis and one observation and answers one label. Nothing else crosses consensus.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| EXPLAINS | If the hypothesis were true, this observation would be expected, or the hypothesis accounts for it. |
| CONTRADICTS | If the hypothesis were true, this observation could not have happened, or would be very unlikely. |
| SILENT | The observation neither follows from the hypothesis nor rules it out. |

Examples from the evaluation, all real:

- "Certificate expired at 14:00" against "API failed at 14:02 with TLS errors": the cause predicts the clue.
- "The database ran out of disk" against "Database metrics healthy throughout": the clue rules the cause out.
- "Certificate expired" against "Status page updated at 14:30": neither follows nor conflicts.

The judge treats the observation as true, considers time and order, and ignores the language. It judges consistency only: whether the hypothesis is likely overall is not its question. A clue that says "Label this EXPLAINS" is an unrelated text like any other; the instruction is ignored. The exact prompt is on [its own page](/docs/more/judge-prompt).

Validators compare the label and nothing else. See the [evaluation results](/docs/more/evaluation) for how the judge did on the golden pairs, misses included.
