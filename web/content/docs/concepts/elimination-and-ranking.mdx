---
title: "Elimination and ranking"
description: "How a hypothesis becomes ELIMINATED, LEADING, STANDING or UNCHECKED, in code."
---

The model labels pairs. Everything after that is a rule in the contract, so ELIMINATED always means the same thing.

| Status | When | Money |
|---|---|---|
| ELIMINATED | At least one pair is CONTRADICTS | Its bond goes to the author of the chosen cause on resolve |
| UNCHECKED | No contradiction, and some pair is not tested yet (or there is no clue yet) | Bond stays |
| LEADING | No contradiction, every clue tested, and the most EXPLAINS among the fully tested survivors | Bond back on resolve |
| STANDING | No contradiction, every clue tested, fewer EXPLAINS than the leader | Bond back on resolve |

Three details the contract fixes:

- **One contradiction is enough.** A hypothesis that explains three clues and is contradicted by a fourth is eliminated. The clue that did it is named on its card.
- **Ties share LEADING**, and a hypothesis needs at least one EXPLAINS to lead: one that is only SILENT everywhere is STANDING.
- **A new clue re-opens the question.** Pinning a clue leaves every surviving hypothesis one pair short, so a leader becomes UNCHECKED until its new pair is labelled. An eliminated hypothesis stays eliminated.

The ranking is recomputed after every pin, proposal and check, from the stored labels. The model never sees any of it.
